What the feeds will miss

Dan Moore's author page lists 159 posts; the blog feed carries 156. All three it drops are from 2026, and one of them — Your Token Proves Who You Are, Not What You Own — is among his best.

A reader alone will not see them. Give the author page a look once a month.

Bright Lights has the opposite problem: its feeds work everywhere but hold only ten items each, which at ten articles a month is three weeks of cover. And the “RSS” button in its own header points at a FeedBurner service Google retired in 2021 — take /feed/ or a category feed instead.

What every index answers

6 questions

  • Where do they publish?

    Every channel, marked live, slow or archive, with the feed URL or a note that there isn't one.

  • How often, really?

    Counted, not guessed — posts per year, current gap, days since the last one.

  • What are they on about now?

    The recent run, summarised, so you can tell whether the subject is still yours.

  • What should you read first?

    The canonical pieces, separated from the output never meant for a reader.

  • What should you discount?

    Who pays them, what they are selling, which categories are marketing.

  • News, or understanding?

    Almost nobody is both. The verdict says which one you are subscribing to.
