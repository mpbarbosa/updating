Bright Lights Film Journal

A fifty-two-year-old film journal, still publishing every three days. What it actually covers now, which feed to take, and why the byline matters more here than anywhere else on this site.

“Wide-ranging, literate, fun, progressive articles on any subject related to film” — its own history page

The verdict

one reader's opinion

Worth following — by subject, never by the firehose

2,928 articles online, the oldest from September 1995, and a new one every three days. Gary Morris founded it as a print quarterly in Cincinnati in 1974; it stopped in 1980, came back in 1993, moved online in 1995 and abandoned the quarterly issue model in April 2014 for rolling publication. Almost nothing on this site has that kind of depth behind it.

It is not a news source, and not by accident. In all of 2026 it has published zero film reviews, zero festival reports and zero journal news. What it publishes is essays — a median of about 3,700 words, routinely 9,000 — on films that are thirty, fifty, seventy years old. This month: Cool Hand Luke, Hud, Eyes Wide Shut, Giger's Alien. Subscribe to understand films, not to find out what came out.

The catch is the opposite of Dan Moore's. There are feeds for everything here — per category, per author, all verified below — but 2026's 74 articles came from 61 different writers, most of them publishing once and never again. There is no house voice left to follow: the masthead's own critics stopped writing years ago. Pick a subject feed and read the piece, not the publication.

Take a category feed, not the main one

Every feed here carries ten items. At five to ten articles a month that is one to two months of the main feed — fine for a daily reader, lossy if you check in monthly. A category feed covers the same ten-item window but at a fraction of the volume, so it loses nothing and arrives pre-filtered.

Both URL shapes work: the flat /category/horror/feed/ and the hierarchical /category/genres/horror/feed/ return the same thing. Use the flat one.

Essays is the one that tracks what the journal is actually doing in 2026 — nineteen of this year's seventy-four articles, and the label it reaches for when a piece is not pegged to anything. What to discount

who writes it, who pays for it, what is stale

The masthead has stopped writing

The four names that built the journal's reputation have gone quiet. Gary Morris, editor-in-chief and founder, has 322 articles and none since December 2023. Alan Vanneman: 188, none since September 2023. Lesley Chow: 60, none since October 2022. Gordon Thomas: 63, none in 2026. Andrew Grossman is the last one still filing, and he managed one article this year.

So the reputation and the current output belong to different people. The journal is more active than it has been since 2020, and almost none of that activity is from the critics whose work earned it the name.

Sixty-one writers, seventy-four articles

2026's output came from 61 distinct bylines — most of them one article and gone. The site has 745 contributor accounts for 2,928 articles. Expect the standard to swing hard from piece to piece: in the same recent run you get a participant's first-hand account of shooting with Orson Welles and a 2,000-word reading of a streaming series.

Judge the article, not the masthead. The useful habit here is the opposite of following a person: scan the subject line, read the first two paragraphs, and abandon freely.

For news, it is the wrong source entirely

Zero reviews, zero festival reports and zero journal news in 2026. A piece on Cool Hand Luke is not late. This is a library that happens to publish on a schedule.

Ad-funded, and the plumbing shows it

Revenue is advertising, sold by the publisher — a web studio — via an Advertise with BLFJ page. No paywall, no subscription, nothing gated. It also means site upkeep is nobody's day job.

The site is stale in the corners

The About page is still the default WordPress placeholder text. The header links to Google+, dead since 2019, and its “RSS” button goes to a FeedBurner email service Google retired in 2021. None of this touches the writing, but do not read the chrome as a signal of anything.

Academic register, no apology for it

A median of 3,700 words and a ceiling around 9,000, with Nietzsche and Paglia cited in the opening paragraphs. Some pieces read as repurposed seminar papers. That is the deal: it was founded to combine popular and academic styles, and it still swings between them mid-paragraph.

How it describes itself

from the staff and history pages

Founded
1974, Cincinnati, by Gary Morris — a typesetter and newspaper film critic at the time, with a lithographer brother doing the printing.
The brief
Great photos, popular and academic styles combined, humour and progressive politics in the mix. Unchanged since the first issue.
Auteurism, widened
It started as director-as-auteur and expanded to actors, producers, studios, genres — any force in a film with a claim to authorship.
The gap
Stopped in 1980, back in 1993. Long enough that it once split a two-part article across thirteen years.
Bright Lights After Dark
The blog added in 2006, merged back into the main site in the 2014 redesign.
April 2014
The quarterly issue model was retired for rolling publication. Everything before it lives in the 82-issue archive.
Banned Words
Its standing list of critical clichés, maintained as a public service and a threat.
Masthead
Gary Morris, editor-in-chief. Publisher Quimby Melton of Studio Hyperset. Associate editors Lesley Chow, Andrew Grossman, Gordon Thomas and Alan Vanneman.
