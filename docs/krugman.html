The verdict

one reader's opinion

Worth following — but it is two publications, and the feed serves only one of them

793 posts in the 702 days since he relaunched on 11 December 2024: 1.13 a day, 1.32 million words. 370 of them landed in 2026, on 313 of the year's first 316 days. Nothing else indexed here publishes at a tenth of this rate, and he has not missed three consecutive days all year.

Two different things go out under the one name. Weekdays: political commentary, a median of 930 words, free, and that is where the readers are — a median 2,800 reactions and about 500 comments a post. Sundays: a single 2,300-to-4,200-word economics essay, paid, often numbered as a series — Federal Reserve 101, Against Oligarchy I–III, Curing U.S. Health Care I–III, Interest Rates I–II. Every one of 2026's 42 Sunday essays was subscriber-only. Nothing else in the year was.

So the reason to index an economist — tariff incidence, bond markets, whether the AI capital splurge can pay for itself — is the part behind the $7 paywall, and it draws about half the reaction the politics draws. The free feed will keep you current on American politics. It will not teach you economics.

The catch is volume with no filter. One feed, twenty items, roughly sixteen days of cover. There are no sections, no categories and no tags, so there is nothing to thin it with: at 33 posts a month, checking in monthly loses about two fifths of the output and gives you no way to choose which two fifths. The podcast feed is the only deep one on the site.

Take both feeds, or you take the wrong half

The main feed holds twenty items and he files 33 a month, so it is a two-week window on a source that produces for thirty. It is the only feed that carries the writing, and there is no narrower one: no section, category or author feed exists on this publication.

The podcast feed is the opposite — 101 items covering twenty-two months, the full archive — because it only has to carry the 101 audio and video posts. Subscribing to both gets you everything spoken and the last fortnight written. Nothing gets you the back catalogue except the archive page or the API.

What to discount

what is paid, what is promotion, what is not an article

The paywall is exactly the economics

Not approximately. In 2026 he published on 313 days and charged for 42 of them, and all 42 were Sundays carrying a single long essay. The free weekday posts have a median of 930 words; the Sunday essays run 2,268 to 4,228. If you subscribe for the economics teaching, you are paying $7 a month for about four posts.

There are two ways round it, both his own: he unlocks and reposts the occasional paid essay weeks later — four of them in 2026 — and in November 2026 he collected much of the paywalled material into an ebook. Neither is a schedule you can subscribe to.

A third of the feed is not an article

Of 2026's 370 items, 80 are video or audio and 26 are under a hundred words — event announcements, a one-line note that the ebook is out, and five posts Substack generates automatically to thank named readers for watching a live video. Those five are not writing in any sense; they arrive in the feed looking exactly like everything else.

Add the duplicates. On 10 November a post went out blank through a Substack failure and was sent again as “(Fixed)”: both copies are in the feed, 23 minutes apart. Since 5 November, posts have also opened with a paragraph selling the ebook before the piece starts.

It is a column, not a bulletin

99 of 2026's 370 titles name Trump or MAGA; 38 name oligarchy, corruption or Musk; 25 are about tariffs or trade. He is an opinion writer in full polemical voice, and the weekday posts are arguments rather than reports. The economics underneath is checkable; the framing is not neutral and does not pretend to be.

Sixteen days of memory

Twenty items in the only feed that carries the writing, against 33 posts a month. There are no sections, categories or tags on this publication, so no narrower feed exists and no reader can filter it. Miss a fortnight and the gap is unrecoverable except by hand.

Everywhere else, he is quiet

317,000 followers on Bluesky and his own posts stop on 21 August 2026. The Korean translation edition in his own nav bar stalled in June. The YouTube channel has no uploads. The Substack is not one channel among several; it is the whole output.

The reading is not where the audience is

Free political posts take a median 2,798 reactions and 496 comments. The paid Sunday essays take 1,442 and 264 — and the best of them, on tariff incidence and interest rates, sit at the bottom of that range. The crowd is here for the politics. The reason to index him is the other thing.
