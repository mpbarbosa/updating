Dan Moore

The most prolific writer on an identity vendor's blog — and, since 2025, one of the clearest explainers of where OAuth meets AI agents.

Sr. Director, CIAM Strategy & Identity Standards at FusionAuth · @mooreds

The verdict

Worth following, with a filter

He has written 159 posts on the FusionAuth blog since March 2020 — 156 of the 417 items in the blog's feed, more than four times the next most prolific author. For most of that run he was the dev-rel voice of the company: release announcements, customer case studies, conference trailers.

That stopped. He has not written a release announcement since April 2024 or a conference trailer since October 2023, and the explanatory “Education” pieces went from 11% of his output in 2020–22 to 69% in 2025–26. What is left is a roughly fortnightly stream of 1,500–5,000-word explainers on identity standards, now aimed squarely at the unsolved problem of authorizing AI agents.

The catch is mechanical: there is no per-author feed, and the one feed that carries him is the whole company blog. Following him takes a filter, and the filter has a hole in it.

The filter has a hole

The author page lists 159 posts; the blog feed carries 156 of them. The three it drops are all from 2026 — Your Token Proves Who You Are, Not What You Own, Amazon Cognito and FusionAuth Comparison and How to Perform a Bulk Permissions Check in FusionAuth FGA By Permify — and the first of those is one of his better pieces. A feed reader alone will miss them, so the author page is still worth a monthly look.

The page also advertises a Markdown version of itself at /blog/author/dan-moore.md. It returns 404 behind a 200-shaped HTML page. Ignore it.

How to read him

what to discount, and what the archive is for

He works for the vendor

Every post lands, sooner or later, on how FusionAuth does the thing. That is mostly harmless in the standards explainers — the OAuth is the OAuth — but it shapes which problems get raised. “Self-hosted beats SaaS” and “single-tenant beats multi-tenant” are also his employer's market position, and the comparison posts against Cognito, Auth0 and Firebase are competitive marketing with the serial numbers filed off.

The tell is the category label. Education is the signal. Community is a customer testimonial, Release Updates is a changelog, Events is a booth announcement, and Compare is a sales document. Filter to Education and you lose almost nothing of value — it is 20 of his last 29 posts.

For news, he is the wrong source

A fortnightly explainer cadence means he is weeks behind an announcement. He is where you go to understand a standard, not to learn it exists.

The 2020–22 archive needs triage

Two thirds of the 159 posts are from his dev-rel years. Framework tutorials from 2020 are stale and the case studies were never for you — but the pattern pieces from that era held up.

His personal blog is a different source

Monthly, no identity content, no employer. Engineering management, careers, and sceptical pieces on AI. Subscribe to it separately or not at all.

Letters To A New Developer is closed

Years of advice for people early in a software career, ending in September 2023. A finished archive, which is its own kind of useful.

His vocabulary

the terms his recent writing assumes you know

CIAM vs IAM
Customer identity versus workforce identity. Different scale, different threat model, different product.
authN vs authZ
Who you are versus what you may do. His recurring point is that the second one is where systems actually fail.
Agentic identity
Giving an AI agent a principal of its own. He treats it as the easy half of the problem.
Token exchange
RFC 8693. Trading a token you hold for one that fits where you are going.
Delegation vs impersonation
Acting on someone's behalf as yourself, versus acting as them. The distinction agent chains live or die on.
CIMD
Client ID Metadata Document. A client identified by a URL that describes it, instead of by prior registration.
DCR
Dynamic Client Registration. Clients enrolling themselves at runtime — the nightclub door.
FGA
Fine-grained authorization. Permissions per object rather than per role.
BFF
Backend for frontend. A server that holds the tokens so the browser never has to.
FedCM
Federated Credential Management. The browser-mediated replacement for cookie-based federated login.
SCIM
The standard for provisioning users between systems. Unglamorous, and what enterprise deals hinge on.
GNAP
Grant Negotiation and Authorization Protocol. The clean-slate OAuth successor he was enthusiastic about in 2021.
B2B2C / B2B2E
Your customer's customers, versus your customer's employees.
Auth facade
His own term: one front door presented over many upstream identity providers.
